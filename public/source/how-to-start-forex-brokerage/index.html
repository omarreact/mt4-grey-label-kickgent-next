The Short Version

To start a forex brokerage on a grey label, you'll need six things working together: a legal entity, a trading platform, a CRM, a way to execute trades, a way to take payments and a website. Get those in place, connect them properly, test the whole journey and you have a brokerage ready to open its doors.

The rest of this page explains each piece and how they fit.

02 / 11

Why a Grey Label Is the Usual Starting Point

There are three broad ways to put MetaTrader in front of clients.

You could hold a platform licence yourself and run your own server. That's expensive, slow to arrange and, for MT4, no longer possible for new applicants. You could take a white label, which gives deeper branding and control but costs considerably more and has become harder to secure. Or you could start on a grey label, operating your brand on a licence holder's server with manager access to your own clients and trading conditions.

For a first brokerage, grey label tends to win on cost, speed and practicality. It lets you prove the business before committing to heavier infrastructure. Our MT4 grey label and MT5 grey label pages explain each in detail.

03 / 11

The Technology Stack, Piece by Piece

Trading Platform

This is where your clients analyse markets and place trades. On a grey label, you'll offer MT4, MT5 or both. You control client groups, leverage, spreads and account settings through your manager account.

CRM and Client Portal

The CRM is your back office. It captures registrations, runs identity checks, opens trading accounts through the manager API, records deposits and withdrawals, manages IB partners and produces reports. The client portal, or trader's room, is the part your traders use to verify, fund and manage their accounts.

Liquidity and Bridge

If client trades are going to the market, a bridge connects your platform to liquidity providers and applies your routing rules. Some grey label arrangements include execution through the licence holder, while others allow your own bridge.

Payments

Payment service providers process deposits and withdrawals. Integrated with your CRM, they credit trading accounts automatically.

Website

Your website introduces your brokerage, explains accounts and conditions, carries legal pages and sends new traders into registration.

Hosting

Your CRM, website and any extra tools need reliable hosting with monitoring and backups. On a grey label, the trading server itself is run by the licence holder.

Optional Extras

PAMM and MAM plugins for money managers, extra languages, prop firm tools and more can be added at launch or later.

04 / 11

MT4 or MT5: Making the Platform Choice

This decision deserves a little time, because it shapes your audience.

MT4 remains popular with traders who've used it for years, especially those running expert advisors written in MQL4. If your target clients are experienced forex traders with established tools, MT4 matters.

MT5 covers a wider range of markets, including exchange-traded instruments, and offers more timeframes, more order types and both hedging and netting accounts. It's where the platform vendor focuses new development. If you plan to expand beyond forex or attract newer traders, MT5 makes sense.

Both is a common answer. Running MT4 and MT5 side by side lets traders choose, and your CRM can manage accounts on both.

Whichever you choose, confirm grey label availability and terms in writing before building around it.

The Launch Stages

Groundwork

Define who your clients are, where they live, what they'll trade and how you'll earn. Decide whether trades will be passed to the market, handled internally or a mix. These answers drive everything that follows.

Company and Regulatory Position

Set up a legal entity to contract with your technology and payment partners. Work out, with legal advice, whether your business model and target markets require a licence. Registering a company isn't the same as being licensed, and regulatory requirements vary by jurisdiction.

Platform Setup

Agree your grey label terms, then configure groups, symbols, leverage, spreads and branding on your manager account.

CRM Configuration

Set up account types, KYC steps, approval rules, staff roles and IB structure, then connect the CRM to MT4 or MT5.

Liquidity

Connect your bridge and providers, or confirm how execution works through your grey label arrangement. Set routing rules that match your risk approach.

Payments

Apply to payment providers early, since approval often takes longer than the technology. Once approved, they're integrated with the CRM. Approval is always the provider's decision.

Website

Build a site that connects registration and login to the CRM and offers platform downloads. Make sure legal and risk pages reflect your actual position.

Testing

Run test clients through registration, verification, deposit, trading and withdrawal. Check IB tracking, emails and every payment method.

Launch and Support

Go live, start marketing and lean on support while your team gets used to the systems.

How the Pieces Talk to Each Other

Here's the client journey once everything is connected.

A trader lands on your website and registers. The CRM records them as a lead. They upload documents in the trader's room, and compliance approves them. The CRM opens their MT4 or MT5 account through the manager API and emails their login.

They deposit through an integrated payment provider. The CRM credits their trading balance. They download the platform from your site and trade. Orders route through your bridge or the grey label execution arrangement. Later, they request a withdrawal, finance approves it, and the balance updates.

When any link is missing, someone on your team fills the gap manually. When everything's connected, the journey runs largely on its own.

Mistakes First-Time Founders Make

  • Picking a jurisdiction before knowing where clients will come from
  • Building around a platform before confirming grey label terms
  • Launching without a CRM and handling accounts by hand
  • Waiting until the end to apply to payment providers
  • Choosing a website that isn't connected to the CRM
  • Ignoring liquidity until clients start complaining about fills
  • Underestimating the support needed in the first few months

Frequently Asked Questions

What do I need to start a forex brokerage?+

A legal entity, a trading platform, a CRM, an execution setup, payment providers and a website, all connected. Depending on your model and markets, you may also need a licence.

Should I choose MT4 or MT5?+

MT4 suits traders attached to its interface and MQL4 tools. MT5 suits brokers expanding beyond forex or attracting newer traders. Many brokers offer both.

Do I need my own liquidity provider?+

It depends on your grey label arrangement and execution model. Some arrangements include execution, while others let you connect your own providers.

Can I start without a CRM?+

Technically, yes, but it quickly becomes unmanageable. A CRM automates onboarding, account creation and payments.

How long does it take to launch?+

The technology can often be ready sooner than the business side. Company setup, licensing and payment provider approval usually set the timeline.

Do I need a licence?+

It depends on your business model and where your clients are. Regulatory requirements vary by jurisdiction, so take legal advice.

Can I upgrade later?+

Yes. You can add MT5, plugins or more providers, and move to white label if one becomes available and makes sense.
