What Is a Forex Liquidity Bridge?

MetaTrader is excellent at showing prices and taking orders. On its own, though, it doesn't connect to the wider market. Something has to sit between your platform and the banks, prime brokers or aggregators supplying prices and filling trades.

That something is a forex liquidity bridge. It works a bit like air traffic control. Prices fly in from your providers, the bridge organises them and passes them to MT4 or MT5. Orders fly out from your traders, and the bridge directs each one to the right destination based on rules you've set, then confirms the result back to the platform.

When it's working well, nobody notices it. When it isn't, traders notice straight away through slow fills, rejected orders or prices that freeze.

To be clear about our part: we set up and configure bridge technology and help you connect to liquidity providers. We aren't a liquidity provider ourselves, and pricing and terms are agreed directly between you and each provider.

  • Monitoring of latency, rejections and connection health
04 / 12

How It Works

Prices In

Your providers stream prices to the bridge. If there's more than one, the bridge combines them into a single feed, applies any markup you've chosen and sends the result to MT4 or MT5.

Orders Out

A trader clicks buy or sell. The platform hands the order to the bridge, which checks your rules. Depending on the client group, instrument or size, the order might go straight to the best available provider, be kept internal or be split.

Confirmation Back

The fill returns through the bridge to the platform, and the trader sees their position. The whole round trip usually takes a fraction of a second.

You set the rules. The bridge applies them consistently, trade after trade.

Use Cases

A new broker on MT4 wants every trade passed to the market while volumes are low. A single provider connection with simple routing does the job.

A broker running MT4 and MT5 wants both platforms using the same providers and rules. One bridge setup covers them.

A broker identifies a group of consistently profitable clients. Routing rules send those accounts to the market, while other flow is handled internally.

A broker loses an afternoon of trading during a provider outage. Adding a second provider with failover reduces the chance of it happening again.

Reliability and Support

Execution issues tend to appear when markets move fastest, around major news or session opens. Monitoring and failover are as important as the original setup.

Access to routing and markup settings should be limited to your dealing and risk staff, with changes logged.

After launch, we help with new instruments, extra providers, rule changes and troubleshooting. Fill quality and liquidity availability depend on market conditions and your providers, so they can't be guaranteed, but we'll help make sure your setup does its part well.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you supply the liquidity?+

No. We configure bridge technology and help you connect to liquidity providers. Terms come from your own agreements with them.

Can one bridge connect MT4 and MT5?+

In many setups, yes, depending on the bridge software and your platform arrangements.

Can I use my own bridge on a grey label?+

It depends on the arrangement. Some include execution through the licence holder, while others allow your own connection.

How many liquidity providers can I connect?+

That depends on the bridge software. Multiple providers allow price aggregation and failover.

Can I apply my own markups?+

Yes. Markups can usually be set by instrument or client group.

Will a bridge improve my execution?+

It gives you control and options. Actual execution quality also depends on your providers, market conditions and configuration.
